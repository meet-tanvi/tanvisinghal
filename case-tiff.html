Turning industry conversations into opportunities at TIFF: The Market

Moonlite Labs · Integrated marketing · Event marketing · Brand activation · Media relations · Business development · Sep 2026

When TIFF announced its inaugural industry market, I recognized an opportunity to introduce Moonlite Labs to filmmakers, producers, studios and other film-industry professionals. I initiated contact with the organizers, secured an exhibitor opportunity and led the company's seven-day exhibition and integrated marketing campaign.

Challenge

Generative AI was, and remains, a contentious subject in the film industry, raising concerns about creative control, intellectual property and its impact on filmmaking. Introducing Moonlite Labs to this audience required more than demonstrating technological capabilities. My task was to establish credibility, address industry concerns and show filmmakers and studios how the platform could support their creative workflows.

TIFF: The Market was also being held for the first time, with 238 exhibitors and no previous edition to benchmark. Without established insights into audience behaviour or event operations, I needed to develop a differentiated marketing strategy, coordinate closely with organizers and vendors, and remain adaptable as new information and opportunities emerged throughout the seven-day market.

My role

As Moonlite Labs' Marketing, Communications and Partnerships Lead, I managed the project end to end, from research, campaign strategy and creative direction to integrated communications, stakeholder and vendor management, on-site activations and post-event follow-up.

Strategy and approach

Research-led positioning and messaging

I reviewed industry research, including McKinsey's 2026 report on AI in film and television production, to understand emerging adoption patterns and the concerns shaping industry attitudes.

The research highlighted practical applications in pre-production, including concept development, storyboarding and visualization, alongside growing opportunities in post-production. I used these insights to position Moonlite Labs around relevant filmmaking workflows, focusing on practical use cases and creative possibilities rather than broad claims about AI.

I applied this messaging across the exhibition visuals, booth experience, email campaigns, editorial content and media communications, and used it in my own conversations with filmmakers and studios. After TIFF, I continued to build Moonlite Labs' marketing communications on the same foundation to show the platform's relevance to professional filmmakers.

Script-to-Screen: turning audience insights into experiential marketing

After several days of speaking with filmmakers, screenwriters and producers, I identified an opportunity to offer something more relevant than a conventional product demonstration. I developed Script-to-Screen, inviting visitors to bring a 30-second script excerpt and see it transformed into visual scenes using Moonlite Labs.

I designed the experience to offer filmmakers immediate creative value and to open conversations about their workflows, their projects and how the platform could apply to their work.

I promoted the activation to TIFF delegates through email outreach, generating advance bookings at our booth. Originally planned for one day, it continued into the following day because of strong demand.

Creator and partner showcase: building community and brand advocacy

Ahead of TIFF, I conceived a creator showcase to celebrate work produced using Moonlite Labs and strengthen relationships with existing users. I invited submissions through LinkedIn and our newsletter, offering selected creators an opportunity to showcase their work before an international film-industry audience.

The initiative demonstrated the platform's creative possibilities through authentic user work while recognizing participating creators and encouraging community engagement. I coordinated the showcase, secured written permissions for footage and brand assets, and established attribution standards.

Integrated campaign execution

Alongside these initiatives, I managed the wider exhibition and marketing programme, coordinating with TIFF organizers, internal teams and vendors while adapting to developments throughout the event.

  • Event marketing and brand activation. Directed booth positioning, exhibition graphics, signage, video content and the visitor experience.
  • Integrated communications. Developed delegate email campaigns, social media content and blog articles to build awareness, encourage visits and communicate practical filmmaking applications.
  • Stakeholder management. Coordinated with organizers, creators, partners and vendors, managing requirements, content permissions and on-site delivery.
  • Media relations and executive visibility. Coordinated the Founder and CEO's CBC News interview and facilitated a visit by Canada's Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture.
  • Business development. Engaged with filmmakers, producers and studios, exploring potential applications and supporting follow-up conversations with prospective customers.

Outcomes

  • 63% average email open rate. The three delegate email campaigns I wrote for market week achieved an average open rate of 63%.
  • Strong demand for Script-to-Screen. The activation I developed generated advance bookings and was extended into a second day in response to demand.
  • Media and industry visibility. Moonlite Labs received coverage from CBC News, through the interview I coordinated with our Founder and CEO, and from Film Picnic. I also facilitated a visit to our stand by Canada's Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture.
  • New business opportunities. The conversations I held with filmmakers, production companies and international studios led to follow-up meetings, product demonstrations and exploratory collaboration discussions.
  • Creator and community engagement. The showcase I conceived brought existing users' work to an international film-industry audience, recognizing creators while demonstrating the platform's creative applications.

What I took forward

What I enjoyed most was the opportunity to connect industry research with real conversations. Speaking directly with filmmakers gave me a better understanding of their creative ambitions, reservations about AI and the applications they found most relevant. It allowed me to test our messaging against their perspectives and refine our approach as the market unfolded.

I particularly valued the freedom to experiment, respond to what I was learning and translate those insights into marketing decisions. That understanding of the audience continued to shape how I approached Moonlite Labs' positioning and communications after TIFF.
