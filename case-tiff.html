Moonlite Labs · September 2026 · Toronto, Canada

Turning industry conversations into opportunities at TIFF: The Market

  • Integrated marketing
  • Event marketing
  • Brand activation
  • Media relations
  • Business development

Introduction

In September 2026, the Toronto International Film Festival opened TIFF: The Market, its first industry market. It brought together 238 exhibitors and filmmakers, producers, studios and other professionals from around the globe, which made it a rare chance to meet the people our platform was built for in one place.

Moonlite Labs decided to exhibit at The Market. As Marketing, Communications and Partnerships Lead at Moonlite Labs, I was responsible for the success of our participation and had complete ownership of the company's seven-day presence at the market, including integrated marketing, event marketing, brand activation, business development and stakeholder relations.

The challenge

Moonlite Labs is an AI platform for video, audio and image generation. At The Market, we would share the space with filmmakers from around the world at a time when generative AI was, and remains, a contentious subject in the film industry, raising concerns about creative control, intellectual property and its impact on filmmaking. As an AI company, we could expect many of the people we wanted to reach to arrive at our booth sceptical.

Introducing Moonlite Labs to this audience required more than demonstrating technological capabilities. My task was to establish credibility, address industry concerns and show filmmakers and studios how the platform could support their creative workflows.

Contentious subjectCreative control, intellectual property and the impact of AI on filmmaking
Inaugural edition of TIFF: The MarketA first for everybody

TIFF: The Market was also being held for the first time. Without established insights into audience behaviour or event operations, I needed to develop a differentiated marketing strategy, coordinate closely with organizers and vendors, and remain adaptable as new information and opportunities emerged throughout the seven-day market.

My role

As Moonlite Labs' Marketing, Communications and Partnerships Lead, I managed the project end to end, from research, campaign strategy and creative direction to integrated communications, stakeholder and vendor management, on-site activations and post-event follow-up.

Strategy and approach

Research-led positioning and messaging

Moonlite Labs' positioning had largely centred on generating content. As the person responsible for our booth messaging and branding, I wanted to understand how film studios actually prefer to use AI before TIFF, so I reviewed industry research, including McKinsey's 2026 report on AI in film and television production, on emerging adoption patterns and the concerns shaping industry attitudes.

The research showed that studios saw the most practical value in pre-production, including concept development, storyboarding and visualization, alongside growing opportunities in post-production. Based on this, I broadened our messaging beyond generating scenes for the final film to include pre-production and post-production, positioning Moonlite Labs around the workflows studios were most ready to adopt.

The research also strengthened my own and the team's vocabulary, so we could converse more fluently with filmmakers, understand their work and support them better.

I applied this messaging across the exhibition visuals, booth experience, email campaigns, editorial content and media communications, and used it in my own conversations with filmmakers and studios. After TIFF, I continued to build Moonlite Labs' marketing communications on the same foundation to show the platform's relevance to professional filmmakers.

Script-to-Screen: turning audience insights into experiential marketing

After several days of speaking with filmmakers, screenwriters and producers, we identified an opportunity to offer something more relevant than a conventional product demonstration.

  1. Bring a scriptVisitors brought a 30-second script excerpt
  2. See it on screenWe transformed it into visual scenes using Moonlite Labs
  3. Talk workflowsConversations about their projects and potential applications

I designed the experience to offer filmmakers immediate creative value and to open conversations about their workflows, their projects and how the platform could apply to their work.

I promoted the activation to TIFF delegates through email outreach, generating advance bookings at our booth. Originally planned for one day, it continued into the following day because of strong demand.

Creator and partner showcase: building community and brand advocacy

Ahead of TIFF, I conceived a creator showcase to celebrate work produced using Moonlite Labs and strengthen relationships with existing users.

I invited submissions through LinkedIn and our newsletter, offering selected creators an opportunity to showcase their work before an international film-industry audience.

The initiative demonstrated the platform's creative possibilities through authentic user work while recognizing participating creators and encouraging community engagement. I coordinated the showcase, secured written permissions for footage and brand assets, and established attribution standards.

Integrated campaign execution

Alongside these initiatives, I managed the wider exhibition and marketing programme, coordinating with TIFF organizers, internal teams and vendors while adapting to developments throughout the event.

Business development

Media and industry visibility

New business opportunities

Conversations with filmmakers, production companies and international studios led to follow-up meetings, product demonstrations and exploratory collaboration discussions.

Creator and community engagement

The showcase brought existing users' work to an international film-industry audience, recognizing creators while demonstrating the platform's creative applications.

What I took forward

What I enjoyed most was the opportunity to connect industry research with real conversations.

Speaking directly with filmmakers gave me a better understanding of their creative ambitions, reservations about AI and the applications they found most relevant. It allowed me to test our messaging against their perspectives and refine our approach as the market unfolded.

I particularly valued the freedom to experiment, respond to what I was learning and translate those insights into marketing decisions. That understanding of the audience continued to shape how I approached Moonlite Labs' positioning and communications after TIFF.
